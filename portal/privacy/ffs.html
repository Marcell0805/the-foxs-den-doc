2. Licence, not a sale

We give you a personal, non-exclusive, non-transferable licence to use the app. We keep the app, the name F.F.S, the fox mark, and the software. You may not copy the app for others, resell it, or reverse engineer it except where the law allows.

3. What F.F.S is

F.F.S is a local record of money in, money out, a monthly budget, goals, and reports on this phone. It is not a bank, a wallet, a payment service, a financial or investment adviser, an accountant, a tax adviser, a credit provider, or a legal adviser. It does not log into your bank, store your banking password or card PIN, or move money. Your bank's own records remain the authority for the real account.

4. What the numbers are

Budgets, reports, payday totals, and goal progress are calculations from the records on this device. They are not a bank statement and they are not advice. F.F.S does not produce a financial forecast. A comparison with an earlier period is a comparison of recorded figures, not a prediction.

5. You check the records

You are responsible for checking anything you type, anything captured from a notification, anything imported from a statement, and anything filled in from a receipt photo before you rely on it. Merchant names, amounts, dates, direction, categories, and duplicate matching can be wrong.

If a Wallet alert and a bank alert are merged into one payment, you still check that row.

A Captured payment from a bank that is switched on can appear in budgets and reports before you accept it. A payment from a bank that is switched off is stored as Unlisted and is left out of those totals. Turning that bank on moves it to Needs review. It does not add it to the totals. It is included when you accept it. A payment you mark as Ignored is left out of those totals.

6. Notification capture

Notification access is optional. You turn it on in Android settings, and you can turn it off there. That Android permission can cover notifications generally. F.F.S keeps payment alerts from supported bank and Google Wallet apps and does not turn other notifications into payments. Inside F.F.S you can switch individual banks and Google Wallet on or off.

F.F.S stores, on this phone, the notification information it needs to process and record those supported payment alerts. It does not ask for your online-banking login.

7. Statements and receipts

You can import a Capitec PDF, a PDF that contains text, or a CSV. A PDF that is only a picture of a page may not be read. You review the rows and confirm them before they are saved as confirmed transactions. You can roll back a confirmed import.

The free import covers roughly the last 92 days of statement history, one import, and one account. That limit is about which statement history can be imported. It does not mean F.F.S deletes older records already on the phone.

A receipt photo is read on the phone to pre-fill a transaction. It is saved only when you save the form. Parsing and text recognition can miss or misread a line.

8. Where your records live

Your financial records stay in a database on this device. We do not run an account or a backup for that database. Android backup of the app is turned off. F.F.S does not sync your financial ledger to a server.

Uninstalling the app, clearing its storage, or using Wipe all data deletes the local records. If the phone is lost or damaged, you may permanently lose access to records that were stored only on that phone.

Export data (CSV) builds a file and hands it to the Android share sheet. You choose where it goes. That file is a copy of what is stored, including anything you have not corrected.

The optional app lock uses your fingerprint, face, or your phone PIN, pattern, or password when you open F.F.S and when you leave it. That is a lock on the app. It is not encryption of the database file.

9. Tools that change your data

These tools are for testing and sample data. They are not part of your bank.

Load screenshot demo replaces your transactions, goals, and budgets with sample data. That replacement cannot be undone. Simulators can add payments on this phone that look like captured bank alerts. They are not records from your bank. Clear testing data removes testing rows. Wipe all data deletes the local ledger and puts starter accounts back.

10. No promise of uninterrupted or perfect operation

The app is provided as available. We do not promise that every notification or statement will be read correctly, that totals will be error-free, or that Android or your bank's notification wording will stay the same.

11. Liability

F.F.S does not give financial, investment, tax, accounting, credit, or legal advice. Figures in the app can be wrong. A decision you make from those figures, a missed or merged payment, a bank charge, or the loss of records that existed only on the phone is your responsibility to check before you rely on them.

This notice is from FoxByte Labs for the Android app F.F.S.

2. What this notice covers

F.F.S keeps your financial records on your phone. We do not run an F.F.S account, we do not ask you to log in, and we do not store your ledger on our servers. We do not use an analytics product, and the in-app feedback screen does not upload what you type.

3. Records stored on the phone

The app stores, in a local database on the device, the accounts, transactions, categories, budgets, goals, imports, and merchant-category memory you create or confirm. Amounts are shown in rand.

That database is ordinary local storage. It is not encrypted as a database. Android backup of the app is turned off.

4. Notification access

If you turn on Android notification access, Android can show this app notifications from many apps. F.F.S keeps payment alerts from supported bank apps and Google Wallet. It does not turn other notifications into payments.

For an alert it keeps, it can store the app package name, the notification title and body, the time it was posted, a notification key, and the original notification text with the payment. It does not ask for your online-banking password or card PIN. You can turn notification access off in Android settings, and you can switch individual banks and Google Wallet off inside F.F.S.

5. Statements and receipts

If you import a Capitec PDF, a PDF that contains text, or a CSV, the file is read on the phone so you can review the rows. A PDF that is only a picture of a page may not be read. The free import window is about the last 92 days of statement history, one import, and one account. That is a limit on what can be imported. It is not a rule that deletes older records already on the phone.

If you take or choose a receipt photo, text recognition runs on the phone and pre-fills a transaction. The photo is not sent to us. The transaction is stored only when you save it.

6. Export

Export data (CSV) builds a file on the phone and opens the Android share sheet. You choose where that copy goes. We do not receive it.

7. What can leave the phone

F.F.S does not upload your ledger.

The app can request a version file from the FoxByte Labs website to see if an update exists. That request does not include your transactions. If you installed from Google Play, updates are offered through Play. If you installed another way, you may be offered a link to an APK.

The app may download font files from Google. Those requests are not your financial records.

8. How long records stay, and how they are removed

Records stay on the phone until you delete them, clear the app's storage, use Wipe all data, or uninstall the app. Those actions delete the local records. Load screenshot demo replaces transactions, goals, and budgets with sample data and cannot be undone. Clear testing data removes testing rows.

If the phone is lost or damaged, we cannot restore records that existed only on that phone. There is no F.F.S backup of the ledger.

9. App lock

If you turn the app lock on, the phone's fingerprint, face, or PIN, pattern, or password is used to open F.F.S. That limits who can open the app. It does not encrypt the database file.
